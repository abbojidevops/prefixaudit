One byte can change the bill.

      
A cached prefix is billed at 0.1× on reads — but only while it matches byte-for-byte. One rendered
        timestamp turns every "cached" call into a full-price write plus a full-price read. PrefixAudit models
        that effect from your inputs and public provider economics, and shows the line responsible.

      
Modelled ≠ measured — web cost figures are models, never invoice measurements.

    

  


  
  

    

      

        Research
        
The PrefixAudit cache benchmark.

        
The rules and the numbers on this page come from a structured study of public production
          prompts — not from vibes. Here is exactly what we measured, and what we only model.

Methodology
Each prompt was parsed, its code blocks masked, then scanned by the same 14 deterministic rules that run in this page and the CLI. Findings are positional: a volatile byte near the top of the prefix weighs more than one near the breakpoint. A 261-test suite re-verifies every rule against false-positive fixtures on every build.

        
Corpus
236 system prompts and tool schemas drawn from public production prompt corpora, spanning 16 vendors and product categories. The corpus is a snapshot of what ships to production in the wild. It is not your workload — and we say so wherever it is quoted.

        
Rules
Four critical, five high, five medium: rendered timestamps, request identifiers, per-user personalisation, mutable memory, below-minimum prefixes, ordering, tool churn, missing breakpoint markers, model churn, TTL cadence, retry rebuilds, missing cache instrumentation, unstable serialisation, prefix bloat. Every rule ships its evidence, its fix and its corpus frequency.

        
Provider assumptions
Cache reads at 0.1× of base input across Anthropic, OpenAI, Gemini and DeepSeek; writes at 1.25× (Anthropic 5-minute TTL, OpenAI GPT-5.6+) or 1.0×; minimums 1,024 / 1,024 / 2,048 / 1,024 tokens. All transcribed from public 2026 price pages — and editable in the instrument, because your contract may differ.

        
Pricing methodology
Dollar figures are a model: prefix tokens × request volume × provider multipliers. They estimate billing mechanics; they never measure your invoice. The instrument labels every figure accordingly.

        
Limitations
Token counts are heuristic (±15% against real BPE on our corpus). The corpus over-represents public, English, assistant-style prompts and does not represent all production AI workloads. Cache hit rates are never claimed as measured — only provider billing mechanics and corpus frequencies are stated as fact.

        
Sources
Harness State of AI in FinOps 2026; FinOps Foundation State of FinOps 2026; ProjectDiscovery's prompt-caching engineering writeup; public 2026 price pages of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and DeepSeek; the 236-prompt public corpus study shipped in this repository.
